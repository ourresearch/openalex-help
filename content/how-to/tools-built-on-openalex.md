Lots of people build tools on OpenAlex data. This page lists the ones whose builders have told us about them, grouped by what they do. Each description is the builder's own, lightly shortened. We check that each tool is live and says it uses OpenAlex, but we don't review or endorse them, and we can't help with problems in them: for those, contact the tool's builder.

## Discovery and reference checking

- **[CiteMe](https://citeme.app)**: a citation and reference-verification tool for students and researchers. OpenAlex is its primary search source: it powers both the search and the reference checker, which compares submitted references against real scholarly records and flags the ones that can't be verified.
- **[PubScope](https://pubscope.org)**: helps authors choose and verify journals, with abstract-based journal suggestions, journal profiles with indexing and DOAJ history, and a free reference check that flags retracted or unfindable citations.

## Collaboration networks and maps

- **[CoAuthra](https://coauthra.com)**: a research collaboration explorer powered by OpenAlex. Search any researcher by name or ORCID to map their co-authorship network; no account needed.
- **[MapAlex](https://smartdata.las.ac.cn/openalex-map/)**: a global map of science for OpenAlex, an open system for analyzing OpenAlex data from the National Science Library, Chinese Academy of Sciences.
- **[pubScan](https://pubscan.org)**: explore your scientific co-authorship network and visualize your collaborations across PubMed publications. Fast, mobile-friendly and completely open source.

## Research data and evidence

- **[Egestabase Research](https://egestabase.net/research/)**: an online evidence platform for exploring the scientific literature on recovering and reusing the plant nutrients in human excreta and domestic wastewater, powered by bibliographic data from OpenAlex.
- **[Open Editors Plus](https://openeditors-plus.org)**: the largest open dataset of academic editorial board composition (more than 920,000 positions across 15,000 journals), with editor and journal metrics from OpenAlex. Explore it on the site, or download it (CC0) from [Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19468382).

## What counts as a tool built on OpenAlex?

An application: a live website, app or service that people can use, which gets its data from OpenAlex (through the API, the snapshot or both) and says so. Search and discovery tools, reference checkers, journal finders, dashboards, maps and evidence databases all count.

These don't go on this page:

- Scripts, notebooks and one-off analyses.
- Research studies and papers that use OpenAlex data: they're in OpenAlex itself (next section).
- Sites that aren't live, or that don't say they use OpenAlex. A tool that goes offline comes off the list.

## Where can I find research that uses OpenAlex?

In OpenAlex: here are [the works that cite the OpenAlex paper](https://openalex.org/works?filter=cites:W4229010617), more than 1,300 of them. If you've published research that uses OpenAlex, please [cite us](/how-to/citing-openalex/) so it shows up there too.

## How do I get my tool listed?

[File a ticket](https://openalex.org/contact) with:

- the tool's name and web address;
- a one- or two-sentence description, in your own words;
- the section above it fits best (or a new one).

If it fits the description above, we'll add it and reply with a link to this page.
